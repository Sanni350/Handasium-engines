Applied Loads (excl. self-weight)
Slab Load Helper — convert a slab panel to an equivalent beam UDL
One-way: UDL = slab load × tributary width. Two-way (yield-line method): triangular short-edge beams get w×Lx/3; trapezoidal long-edge beams get w×Lx/2×[1−1/(3β²)], β=Ly/Lx. Repeat for each slab panel feeding this beam — "Add" accumulates onto the current Dead/Live values rather than replacing them.
Self-weight is calculated automatically from geometry × 24 kN/m³ and added to Gk. Up to 3 point loads are analysed exactly (via statics, all combined) for Simply Supported and Cantilever; for continuous conditions they're added as a conservative superposition — verify against a full analysis for point-load-dominated continuous beams. Leave P at 0 for any slot you don't need.
Direct Design Actions
Section design (flexure, shear, deflection, detailing, BBS) runs exactly as normal on your entered Mu/Vu. Self-weight/load analysis and the BMD/SFD are skipped — you already have the real moment/shear distribution from your own analysis.
Moment Redistribution (optional)
Applies the correct (stricter) xu,max/d ductility limit per IS 456 Cl. 37.1.1 for a section where redistribution has been taken. This does NOT reduce Mu — if you redistributed moments in your own analysis, enter the already-reduced Mu via Direct Design Mode. Leave at 0 for standard elastic design.
Torsion (optional)
Tu must come from your own 3D frame/grillage analysis — this planar beam engine cannot derive torsional demand from load inputs alone.

Glossary — what do these terms mean?
Mu / Vu
Design (ultimate, factored) bending moment and shear force — the loads the section must be strong enough to resist, after safety factors are applied.
d (effective depth)
Distance from the compression face of the concrete to the centroid of the tension steel. Bigger d = stronger, more efficient section — it's the single most influential dimension in the design.
xu / xu,max (neutral axis depth)
The depth to the neutral axis of the section under load. xu,max is the code limit on how deep this can go while still ensuring the steel yields before the concrete crushes (ductile failure). If the moment demands more than xu,max allows, compression steel is needed.
Mu,lim
The largest moment a singly-reinforced section (tension steel only) can safely resist at the xu,max limit. If your actual Mu exceeds this, the section becomes doubly reinforced.
Ast / Asc
Ast = area of tension steel, Asc = area of compression steel (only present in doubly-reinforced sections).
Singly vs. doubly reinforced
Singly reinforced = steel only on the tension face. Doubly reinforced = steel on both faces, needed when the section is too shallow to resist the moment with tension steel alone.
Sagging / hogging
Sagging = the beam bends into a "smile" shape, tension on the bottom (typical mid-span). Hogging = bends into a "frown," tension on top (typical over a continuous support).
τc (tau-c)
The shear stress the concrete alone can resist without any stirrups/links. If the applied shear stress τv exceeds τc, shear reinforcement (links) is required.
τc,max (tau-c-max)
The absolute upper limit on shear stress — beyond this, the concrete would crush regardless of how much reinforcement is added. Exceeding it means the section itself must be made bigger.
Asv / sv
Required area of shear links per unit spacing along the beam — used to work out how far apart your stirrups need to be.
Effective span (Leff)
The span actually used in design calculations, derived from your clear span plus an allowance for support width/depth — not simply the distance between wall faces.
Curtailment
Stopping some reinforcing bars short of the full span once they're no longer needed to resist the (reduced) moment there — saves steel, but must be done correctly with enough extra length for development/anchorage.
Development length (Ld)
The length a bar must be embedded in concrete for bond stress to safely transfer its full tensile force — too short, and the bar can pull out before it yields.
Moment redistribution (δm)
A technique used in continuous-beam analysis where peak moments are deliberately reduced at some locations (and increased elsewhere) to reflect real ductile behaviour — it tightens the xu,max/d ductility limit at the redistributed section.
Torsion — equivalent shear/moment (Ve, Me)
IS 456's method for designing beams under combined shear and torsion: torsion is converted into an "equivalent" extra shear and an "equivalent" extra moment, and the beam is then designed for those increased values rather than for torsion directly.
Bearing stress
The compressive stress where the beam sits on its support (wall/column) — too high, and the support material itself could crush or the beam could punch into it.
